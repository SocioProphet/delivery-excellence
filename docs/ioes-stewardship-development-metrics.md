# IOES Stewardship and Development Metrics

## Purpose

This document aligns Delivery Excellence with the IOES stewardship stack.

IOES means Identity, Ontogenesis, Ecology, and Stewardship.

Delivery Excellence should measure whether work produces durable, humane, evidence-backed outcomes without reducing people to productivity objects. Metrics are tools for stewardship, not little numerical tyrants in spreadsheet hats.

## Metric doctrine

Delivery metrics must distinguish outputs, outcomes, learning, stewardship, risk, and human development.

A person is not a KPI.

A team is not a velocity chart.

A learner is not a completion percentage.

A steward is not an owner.

Metrics may guide attention, review, repair, and improvement. Metrics must not become a substitute for judgment, consent, evidence, or human dignity.

## Metric families

### Stewardship debt

Definition: the count and severity of entrusted artifacts, obligations, projects, learning objects, or relationships without adequate keeper coverage.

Signals:

no active keeper

no successor

expired review interval

contested authority

missing evidence refs

missing policy snapshot

stale handoff

High stewardship debt means continuity risk.

### Succession risk

Definition: likelihood that a stewardship object becomes orphaned if the current keeper leaves, fails, or becomes unavailable.

Signals:

single keeper

no apprentice

no successor rule

no emergency authority

no recent review

high knowledge concentration

high operational dependency

### Learning continuity

Definition: whether learners, mentors, teams, and communities retain coherent learning trajectories across work, curriculum, and practice.

Signals:

active learning path

mentor relation present

artifact evidence available

next-best-action traceable

reviewed progress notes

consented learner-continuity claims

missing or broken continuity should trigger repair, not blame.

### Evidence completeness

Definition: degree to which delivery claims are backed by verifiable evidence.

Signals:

artifact refs present

source refs present

policy decision refs present

execution evidence refs present

review records present

replay records present

unsupported claims must remain candidates.

### Developmental progress

Definition: movement through an explicit ontogenesis model such as seed, formation, growth, maturity, transmission, transformation, or archive.

Signals:

phase history

reviewed phase transition

promotion evidence

blocked transition reasons

repair plan

mentor or successor readiness

Developmental progress is not always speed. Sometimes maturity means slowing down before the machinery turns into soup.

### Ecological dependency risk

Definition: exposure created by hidden or fragile dependencies in the Gaia context.

Signals:

critical dependency without steward

external system fragility

resource bottleneck

community dependency

evidence-source dependency

infrastructure dependency

policy or jurisdiction dependency

### Delivery reliability

Definition: ability to produce promised outcomes with evidence, reviewability, and bounded authority.

Signals:

commitment kept

receipt emitted

execution replayable

policy conformance present

customer proof present

repair cycle closed

handoff complete

### Human agency preservation

Definition: whether delivery patterns increase rather than reduce human agency.

Signals:

consent maintained

review options present

appeal or repair path present

explanation available

automation bounded

learning produced

dependency on opaque agent reduced or justified

## Anti-metrics

These must not be used as standalone human value metrics:

hours online

messages sent

tickets closed

agent actions accepted

curriculum completed

velocity points

engagement time

rank score

availability outside agreed cadence

They may be operational signals, but they are not measures of human worth, stewardship, wisdom, or learning.

## Scorecard structure

An IOES-aware delivery scorecard should have separate sections.

Outcome evidence: what was delivered and what proves it.

Policy posture: what policies allowed, denied, repaired, or reviewed.

Stewardship posture: what is now entrusted and to whom.

Succession posture: what happens if the keeper is unavailable.

Learning posture: what was learned, taught, or preserved.

Gaia posture: what dependencies or impacts were surfaced.

Human-agency posture: whether the work increased human capability and consented autonomy.

Risk posture: what remains fragile, unsupported, contested, or stale.
